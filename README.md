# SupremeAnalytics Limited · NumVault

> **Privacy should not be optional by default.**

**SupremeAnalytics Limited** is a Nigerian software company (RC 9856619, incorporated 14 September 2026) founded and directed by **Simon Oluwaferanmi Onabanjo**, a Software Engineering graduate of Lead City University, Ibadan. More at [numvault.cloud/company](https://numvault.cloud/company).

**NumVault** is our first product: a mobile app that gives people a private phone number for any app, so their real number, and the identity tied to it, stays private.

---

## 1. Why the phone number

In Nigeria the phone number is the root key of a person's digital life:

- every app **signs up** with it,
- every password **resets** through it,
- every bank and wallet **confirms payments** with it,
- and every SIM is **linked to the owner's NIN**.

Whoever controls the number controls the identity. NumVault protects that root.

## 3. The problems we solve in Nigeria, at the same time

1. **Privacy and fraud.** A real number tied to your NIN and bank is a target for spam, SIM-swap fraud and data resale. NumVault keeps it out of every sign-up.
2. **Access to foreign platforms.** Demand for US numbers in Nigeria keeps growing: WhatsApp Business lines, freelancing and remote-work platforms, and US services (PayPal, Stripe, Amazon, Uber…) that require a foreign number to register. ([Fanytel](https://fanytel.com/blog/en/us-virtual-number-nigeria), [ViralVibe](https://viralvibe.ng/blog/what-are-foreign-numbers-and-why-do-nigerians-buy-them), [costinnigerianaira](https://www.costinnigerianaira.com/2026/05/how-to-get-us-phone-number-for-remote.html))
3. **Student income.** Some studies put close to half of Nigerians aged 15–24 as jobless or underemployed, and about 96% of employed youth work informally ([AWJAI](https://awjai.org/2026/07/24/youth-unemployment-in-nigeria/), [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/812300/youth-unemployment-rate-in-nigeria/)). A 2026 survey of 4,000+ students across 55 Nigerian institutions found **two-thirds already earn while studying**, a campus gig economy of about **$24m a month** ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/03/16/nigerias-campus-gig-economy-may-be-generating-%E2%82%A6400-billion-a-year/)). Our lead programme turns that habit into a paid job.

## 4. A self-funding asset

NumVault is built so that **money always comes in before it goes out**:

- **Direct purchases:** Paystack splits the payment. The wholesale goes straight to our supplier, Socially.ng; only the ₦1,500 fee lands in our account.
- **Wallet purchases:** after each purchase the app checks the supplier (wholesale) balance and, when it runs low, tops it up from money that has **already settled** in our Paystack balance, after first setting aside everything owed to leads.
- **Lead pay follows revenue:** a lead is paid ₦50,000 when they reach 38 customers (by then those customers have paid us ₦57,000) and ₦50,000 at 76 (₦114,000 collected). Every payout is reviewed before it is sent.
- **A lead's first month is unpaid** (qualification), so every new lead brings in ₦114,000 before we pay them anything.
- **Repeat purchases carry no lead cost:** a lead is paid once per customer. Every later purchase by that customer is the full ₦1,500.

Nobody is ever paid out of pocket. Payouts, top-ups and refunds are all funded by sales that already happened.

## 5. The flywheel

```
students buy numbers ──► some find the job in Profile ──► they become leads
        ▲                                                      │
        │                                                      ▼
 customers buy again ◄── new customers buy ◄── leads share their code
```

We don't push the job on customers; they find it in the Profile tab. Once there, a student sees they can earn **₦600,000**, and the loop keeps turning:

1. A customer buys a number.
2. Some customers become leads and do **one unpaid month** (76 paying customers).
3. They then earn **₦100,000 a round** for **six paid rounds** (76 customers per round).
4. Their customers buy again, and some of *them* become leads.

When Lead City is saturated, leads keep finding customers **outside the university** (friends, siblings and course mates at other campuses, business owners, family), so the flywheel carries NumVault beyond its entry point.

### 5.1 The lead programme, exactly as the code runs it

- **Qualifying (unpaid):** 76 validated customers within 30 days. Progress is saved at 19, 38 and 57; if the 30 days run out, the last checkpoint carries into a new 30 days automatically.
- **Paid rounds:** six rounds of 76 customers. A round ends at **76 customers or 30 days, whichever comes first**, so fast leads finish sooner. **Each lead runs on their own clock** from the day they join.
- **Pay:** ₦50,000 at 38 customers, ₦50,000 at 76. A round that ends early by time is paid pro rata (₦100,000 ÷ 76 ≈ ₦1,316 per customer), and the lead re-qualifies.
- **A customer counts** only after a successful paid purchase, once, with their own email.
- **Contract ends** after six paid rounds.

### 5.2 One lead, start to finish (on target, one month per round)

| Round | Customers | NumVault keeps (₦1,500 each) | Lead is paid | Net for NumVault | Wholesale sent to supplier (at ₦1,422) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qualifying (unpaid) | 76 | ₦114,000 | ₦0 | **+₦114,000** | ₦108,072 |
| Paid round 1 | 76 | ₦114,000 | ₦100,000 | +₦14,000 | ₦108,072 |
| Paid rounds 2–6 (each) | 76 | ₦114,000 | ₦100,000 | +₦14,000 | ₦108,072 |
| **Contract total** | **532** | **₦798,000** | **₦600,000** | **+₦198,000** | **₦756,504** |
| **Every month after** (1 repeat purchase each) | 532 returning | ₦798,000 | ₦0 | **+₦798,000** | ₦756,504 |

Profit exceeds lead pay in **every single round**, and once the contract ends that lead's customers keep paying with no lead cost at all.

**Assumptions** (labelled as assumptions until real sales replace them):
- every lead hits 76 customers per 30 days (their target), each on their own clock;
- **1 in 100** customers becomes a lead, starting 30 days after their first purchase (deliberately slow);
- each customer buys **1 more number a month** after their first month;

## 8. Market size and capacity

**Our 100% target is every Nigerian with a NIN-linked phone.** About **137–142 million** people have a NIN ([TheCable](https://www.thecable.ng/nimc-says-nin-enrolment-crosses-136m-as-new-identity-law-takes-effect/), [Leadership](https://leadership.ng/nimc-enrolment-hits-over-140m-in-three-years-fg/)), and **96% of SIMs (153 million lines)** are linked to a NIN ([Techpoint](https://techpoint.africa/news/nin-sim-linkage-september-2024/)). **Can the system carry it?**
- **Database (Supabase, paid plan):** 100,000 sales a day is 1–2 writes a second; Postgres handles thousands. Server size can be raised as we grow.
- **Payments (Paystack, registered business):** no collection cap once approved; volume discounts above ₦10m a month; bulk transfers of up to 100 per request. Scale Mode splits large top-ups into ₦10m transfers automatically.
- **Supply (Socially.ng):** 2,325 apps on US numbers and ~77 countries on the second server. The supplier refunds us when no code arrives, and we pass that on to customers. Apps sometimes block number ranges; suppliers rotate new ones, and a second supplier is our planned safeguard.
- **More numbers on demand:** new countries and services can be switched on as customers ask for them, with no change to our fee model.
